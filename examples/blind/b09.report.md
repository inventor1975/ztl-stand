## APPROXIMATED
- Annuity law A = P*r(1+r)^n/((1+r)^n-1) is NOT in the format (no powers, and nonlinear in the rate). I computed the factor
  k(r) = r(1+r)^n/((1+r)^n-1) OUTSIDE the stand (Python Fraction, exact, then rounded outward at 1e-12) and wrote the law as
  A == (Price - D)*M.k. For variable products k is a range [k(lowest rate), k(highest rate)]; k is monotone in the rate,
  so the range is a true enclosure. Safer-or-equal (outer enclosure); but the law itself is not stated in the brick,
  only its precomputed values - the stand cannot check my k. The "source" text says so.
- The cap on L4: rate = min(index+1.2%, 6.0%) is not expressible (no min). I used the range of the capped rate, 3.7..6.0%,
  precomputed by hand (monotone in the index, so exact hull). Same status as above.
- "A <= 35% of income": a requirement bound must be given/catalog, not derived, and may not be an expression. I added a derived
  Slack == 35/100*Inc - A and required Slack >= 0. Equivalent.
- Unit EUR is unknown; I used USD as a label for money (dimension is consistent, the label is wrong).
- Objective "worst-case total paid" is not expressible: the objective is evaluated at nominal values. I wrote the worst case
  by hand in the objective: n*khi*(Price - Dmin) + fp*(Price - Dmin) + fx with an extra given Dmin = 60 000 and a second
  field khi = upper end of k. This duplicates two assumptions (Dmin, khi) - unknown risk if they drift from the ranges.
  The exact worst-case Tot is also in judge_log (Tot upper end) but only for the judged designs.

## COULD NOT EXPRESS
- Power / exponent (1+r)^n, and a rate as an uncertain quantity feeding a nonlinear law. Needs: a pow operator with
  integer exponent (n from a catalog field), or a derived quantity monotone-bounded by the stand.
- min/max (cap on a total rate).
- Optimisation of a worst case (minimize the maximum over tolerances) - objective is nominal only.
- A requirement bound that is a derived quantity or an expression (A <= 0.35*Inc directly).
- Currency units (EUR).
- Percentage-of-principal fee is fine; but a fee defined as a choice between "% or flat" per product has no direct form
  (done via two fields fp, fx, one of which is 0).
