# c06 report: Physiological buffer for a cell assay (problem 6)

Verdict: EARNED. Design: phosphate, 18 mL acid-form stock + 40 mL base-form stock, $1.16.
`optimal: false`: one cheaper design is undecided (phosphate 16 + 35 mL, $1.02). By my own corner calculation that
design is feasible, with margin 0.0003 pH.

## EXPRESSED FAITHFULLY
- Four systems as one listed catalog (phosphate, HEPES, Tris, MOPS). Each has pKa(25 C) as a range (+-0.02 or +-0.03)
  and dpKa/dT as an exact value.
- Two integer slots for mL, with stock concentration 1.0 M +-1 % (c1, c2 independent), pipette error +-0.5 mL per
  dispense (e1, e2) and flask 1.00 L +-0.3 % (Vf).
- pKa(T) = pKa25 + dpKa*(T-25), with the ionic-strength shift -(0.06+0.4*I). I is computed from the actual
  moles: phosphate 1 per mol of the acid form and 3 per mol of the base form (Na2HPO4 gives 3 Na+ and HPO4(2-));
  HEPES, MOPS and Tris 1 per mol of the neutralising titrant (NaOH or HCl).
- pH = pKap + log10(nA/nHA), written as a law, with the moles of acid and base forms computed from the dispensed
  volumes and the stock concentrations.
- beta = 2.303*C_tot*Ka*H/(Ka+H)^2 with Ka = 10^-pKap and H = 10^-7.4 (written with `pow`), C_tot = total moles / Vf.
- Ionic strength <= 0.15 M as a top-level requirement.
- Two modes: "at 37C" (pH in 7.35..7.45, beta >= 15) and "temp drift" (T in 35..39 C, pH in 7.30..7.50).
- Objective: stock cost by system price per mL.
- Units are all declared (mol/L, mL, L, 1/C, USD/mL); the stand reported no unit warnings.

## APPROXIMATED
1. **The exact charge-balance version was replaced by Henderson-Hasselbalch** (the problem allows either). I checked
   the size of the difference outside the stand: for phosphate 16/35 mL, exact charge balance with the apparent Ka gives
   7.39794 against 7.39795 for HH (scipy `brentq`, Kw = 1e-14 and 2.4e-14). Negligible. Only I checked this; the stand
   does not know it.
2. **Reparametrised the integer choice.** The design variables are x = mL "acid-form" and y = mL "base-form"
   (what the question asks for); the stock volumes follow by integer coefficients (HEPES/MOPS stock 1 = x+y and
   titrant = y; Tris stock = x+y and HCl = x). This is a bijection of the integer grid, so it is safe. Reason: with
   the raw stock volumes, designs whose acid amount goes negative came out OPEN ("cannot bound ['pH']") instead of
   REFUTED (see DOC GAPS 2). The coefficients are checked by nobody except me.
3. **The catalog was cut to x = 8..30 mL and y = 8..44 mL.** The cut is NOT conservative in principle: the stand never
   saw designs outside it. My own corner enumeration over x = 1..120 and y = 1..120 (before reparametrising) found no
   feasible design outside phosphate 16/35 and 18/40. The first attempt with 60 x 60 x 4 systems = 14 400 designs was killed by the
   900 s limit with NO output at all (see TOOL OUTPUT). The cut keeps both phosphate optima and is far above the
   range where Tris, HEPES and MOPS fail.
4. **The apparent-pKa shift below I = 0.05 M is extrapolated linearly** (-0.06 - 0.4*I). The problem gives it only
   between 0.05 and 0.15. All designs found have I of about 0.12..0.14, so the extrapolation is not used there. Nobody checks it.
5. **Pipette error taken as one +-0.5 mL error per stock dispense**, independent of the volume. If a volume were
   dispensed in several strokes, the error would be larger. The problem does not say.
6. **Prices.** "$0.02 per mL pair" for phosphate was read as $0.02 per mL of each stock dispensed. For HEPES, Tris and
   MOPS only the buffer stock is priced; NaOH and HCl cost 0 (no price given). Interpretation, checked by nobody.
7. **beta evaluated at the same T = 37 C and with the same I shift as pH**, at pH 7.40 exactly (as stated). beta is not required in the drift mode (the problem asks only for pH there).
8. **Water autoionisation, flask and stock-volume additivity** are not modelled beyond item 1. Vf only enters the concentration and I.
9. The 3 mL flask error is a tolerance on Vf (0.3 %) and acts only on concentration, ionic strength and beta.
   It does not touch the pH ratio, as it should.

## COULD NOT EXPRESS
- The exact charge-balance pH (a nonlinear implicit equation in [H+]; the implicit-law feature could in principle do it, but I did not try it at this size).
- Integer choices that differ per system (the volume window that suits phosphate is useless for HEPES). Every system
  must share one integer catalog, so the product 4 x |x| x |y| becomes the whole design count.
- A cost per mL that applies to a derived stock volume (I wrote it as an objective on the slot fields with coefficients, which works, but it is hand algebra).
- "For each system, which volumes pass" as one answer. The stand stops at the first EARNED design in cost order, so
  HEPES/Tris/MOPS were never tested in the main run (they all cost more than $1.16). To see them I had to run a second brick with `"report": "all"` on a window (below).
- The 200-design cap on `designs` hides which design is the undecided one (see TOOL OUTPUT).

## TOOL OUTPUT
- First attempt (raw stock-volume slots, 1..60 each, 14 400 designs): **no output, killed by `timeout 900`**
  (`real 15m0.002s`). The stand prints nothing until the end, so the run showed nothing about progress.
- Small probes: 36 designs (volumes 1..3) took 7.4 s; 100 designs 19.5 s; the first EARNED run (93 designs, 14..20 x 28..42) took 78 s.
- Raw-volume probes showed 75 of 100 designs OPEN with `a definition could not be bounded: cannot bound ['pH']` for
  HEPES, Tris and MOPS designs where the acid or base amount is negative or straddles zero. `valid` ("min(nHA, nA) > 0") did
  not turn them into REFUTED. That is why I reparametrised.
- **Final run** (`examples/blind2/c06.json`, 23 x 37 x 4 = 3 404 designs): `real 7m58.627s`, 739 designs tried, EARNED.
  - Design: phosphate, x = 18 mL, y = 40 mL; objective 29/25 ($1.16); `optimal: false`, "1 cheaper design(s) undecided".
  - at 37C: Ion in [0.134237, 0.141825], pH in [7.35041, 7.44581], beta in [24.589, 33.074].
  - temp drift: pH in [7.34481, 7.45141].
  - The 200 listed designs are all REFUTED. The one undecided design is not among the listed ones, so I identified it in
    a smaller run (14..20 x 28..42, 100 designs): phosphate 16 + 35, OPEN with "pH proved within [7.34821, 7.448], which reaches past the
    bound, yet no corner violates it — the extreme may lie inside the box and no certificate decided it".
- Supplementary run (scratch, not kept): `report: all`, x = 14..19, y = 20..29, 240 designs, `real 2m51s`: all 240
  REFUTED (HEPES, Tris, MOPS and phosphate, none of which fits in that window). `closest_design` was HEPES 18/26 with
  overshoot 0.00034, taken at the stand's witness point.

## DOES THE ANSWER LOOK RIGHT
Checked with my own python (a different code path: corner enumeration of c1, c2, e1, e2, Vf, pKa x T = 35, 37, 39;
the pH is monotone in each, so corners give the true range):
- Phosphate 18/40, true ranges: Ion [0.13424, 0.14183] (stand: same to 6 digits, exact match); pH at 37 C
  [7.35258, 7.44365] (stand's [7.35041, 7.44581] contains it, wider by 0.002, as expected from interval arithmetic
  where I appears twice); drift pH [7.34698, 7.44925] (inside the stand's range); beta [27.23, 29.88] (inside the stand's
  [24.59, 33.07], loose).
- Phosphate 16/35: true pH range [7.35025, 7.44597], so it is **truly feasible by 0.00025 pH**, while the stand says OPEN. The EARNED $1.16 answer is
  therefore sound but not the cheapest in truth; the stand correctly says `optimal: false`.
- My scan over x, y in 1..120: only these two phosphate designs pass all requirements; HEPES, Tris and MOPS best case misses by >= 0.010 pH (HEPES 32/47 gives pH37 [7.3418, 7.4605]).
  The stand itself judged only a small window of those systems, all REFUTED (not the whole range).
- Refuted witnesses look plausible: phosphate 14/28 true pH37 low end 7.317, stand's witness shortfall 0.024
  (witness is a point, not the worst, so it is less than the true 0.033); phosphate 15/28 drift low end 7.282, witness shortfall
  0.0067 (true 0.018).
- Not checked: the stand's checker agreement with my units conversion beyond the numbers above; the cost objective value was only checked
  by reading it (18+40 = 58 mL x $0.02 = $1.16, correct).

## DOC GAPS
1. A dimensionless quantity's `"unit": ""` is used in examples (t13) but BRICKS.md never says so. I copied it.
2. BRICKS.md says a law's `valid` condition becomes a requirement. In my case (log10 of a possibly negative ratio)
   the design came out OPEN with "cannot bound", not REFUTED; the doc does not say that a log/ln of an argument whose
   interval straddles zero or is negative cannot be judged before `valid` is applied. A negative-argument design should be refuted by `valid`.
3. Nothing in BRICKS.md warns about run time per design. I measured 0.2 s (trivial) to ~0.85 s (nonlinear definition with
   two modes) per design, so a catalog of a few thousand designs takes ~10 min, and 14 000 does not finish in 900 s. And a timeout loses all output.
4. The `designs` lines show `pKa=[..], dpKa=..` but not the item's label field (`name`), so I could tell systems apart only by their numbers.
   The final `design` block does show the label.
5. "`optimal: false` — 1 cheaper design undecided" does not say which one, and `designs` is capped at 200, so the user cannot find it. (The doc mentions the cap but not this consequence.)
6. `closest_design` ranks by the overshoot at the witness point, not by how far the design is from passing. For
   HEPES 18/26 the witness shortfall is 0.0003 but the true worst case is 0.020 away; "closest" reads as "almost passes". The doc says "not necessarily the worst" only for `by`.
7. Whether two slots may share one integer catalog is not stated. It works (my first 3 x 3 probe used one shared catalog); I split them into mLA and mLB later only to give the slots different ranges.
8. `"integers"` needs a `unit`; it is shown as `""` in the guide, but a unit like `mL` works and is checked.
