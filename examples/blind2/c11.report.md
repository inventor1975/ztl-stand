# c11 report: Supply-air duct and fan selection (problem 11)

Final brick: `examples/blind2/c11.json` (15 designs = 5 ducts x 3 fans, generated by a script of mine, plain JSON).
Verdict on the final brick: **REFUTED** — no duct/fan pair meets the 8 m/s velocity limit at 4400 m3/h; the largest
duct (400 mm) gives 9.73 m/s. Because of that, the "which pair is cheapest" question has no answer as stated. To get
the rest of the answer I ran relaxed variants (velocity limit 10 m/s) in my scratchpad — see TOOL OUTPUT.

## EXPRESSED FAITHFULLY
- Duct catalog (5 diameters, USD/m) and fan catalog (3 fans, price, eta) as listed items; slots DUCT and FAN.
- Fan curves: each is concave piecewise-linear (checked in the generator by an assert), so the curve is exactly
  `min` of its four segment lines. Each fan carries 4 lines (c_i, s_i) as catalog fields. No fitting for the main brick.
- Air: `rho = 353/(T+273)`, T range 10..40 C; kinematic viscosity linear in T through the two given points
  (1.46e-5 at 15, 1.7e-5 at 40, extrapolated linearly down to 10 C). T is ONE given range feeding rho, nu and the fan
  scaling, so their correlation is kept.
- Pressure drop `(f_G*Lg/D + f_F*Lf/D + SumK)*rho*v^2/2`, with SumK = 4*0.3 + damper + 0.5 + 1.5 (diffuser on duct velocity).
- Re = v*D/nu per duct and flow; roughness 0.15 mm (galvanised) and 0.9 mm +-50 % (flexible), as `tolerance 0.5`.
- Flow band 3600..4400 m3/h as a given range; velocity requirement `vmax = Qd/A <= 8` is judged over the whole range.
- Power at nominal `Q*dp/eta`, 1.6 kW limit; margin requirement at 4000 m3/h (fan pressure - 1.1 x system dp >= 0).
- Lifetime cost `price*38 m + fan price + 40000 h * 0.15 USD/kWh * P`, as a worst-case objective over the box.
- Units checked on every law ("checked: every law's dimensions agree").

## APPROXIMATED
1. **Colebrook is not implicit in the brick.** The format has no second implicit quantity ("one implicit law per
   quantity", and an implicit law may use no derived quantity except Q). I wrote the iteration the problem itself
   mentions: Swamee-Jain start, then two Colebrook fixed-point steps, nested in `define`. My offline check (python,
   5 iterations further) shows the 2-step value within about 1e-5 relative of converged Colebrook over D 250/400,
   Re 5e4..5e5, eps 0.15/1.35 mm. The truncation error is NOT bounded inside the stand; only I checked it, at 12
   sample points. Not conservative by construction (error is tiny but unsigned).
2. **"Fan delivers >= 3600 m3/h" done by hand algebra, not by the operating point.** The fan curve falls with Q and
   the system curve rises, so the crossing is >= 3600 iff fan(3600) >= system(3600). I wrote the derived margin
   `m36 = fan(3600)*rho/rho20 - dp(3600) >= 0` instead of intersecting. The monotonicity argument is mine; the stand
   does not check it. Reason: `min` inside an implicit law is refused (see TOOL OUTPUT), and the alternative was a
   smooth fan polynomial. In the final brick the operating-point flow Q is therefore **not computed or reported**.
3. **Fan pressure at other temperatures** scaled by `rho/rho20` (fan law, pressure proportional to density at fixed
   volume flow). The problem says only "at 20 C". My assumption; nobody checks it.
4. **Damper**: "K = 1.2 but at least 60 % open, K = 0.4 fully open" read as a given range K in [0.4, 1.2] that the design
   must survive for every value. This is conservative (the designer would really pick a setting), changes the
   answer only mildly (K enters ~4% of the loss at 4 m/s), and is a reading nobody checks.
5. **Length**: 38 m total = 28 m galvanised + 10 m flexible (the statement could also be 38 + 10). Priced as 38 m of
   duct at the same USD/m for flexible. My reading.
6. **Power and "nominal point"**: `Pw = 4000 m3/h * dp_system(4000) / eta` (the pressure the system needs at 4000), not
   fan-curve pressure at the real operating point. Lifetime cost uses this `Pw`. My reading; the real operating point
   is above 4000 for most designs, so this under-states running power for strong fans (not conservative for cost
   ranking; irrelevant to the REFUTED verdict).
7. **Velocity "at the highest flow"** = 4400 m3/h from the demand band, not at the fan's operating point (which can
   be >4400, e.g. 400 mm + F2 about 5300-5550 m3/h in my offline check). Not conservative if the operating-point flow
   was meant.
8. Numbers like `1.46e-5` written as `0.0000146`; `pi` is the stand's 1e-15 bracket; `T0 = 273` as a given with unit C.

## COULD NOT EXPRESS
- The operating point itself, together with the true piecewise-linear fan curve. `min` (the exact form of a concave
  curve) crashes the implicit-law machinery; the only workable form is a smooth fan curve (see below), which is not
  the vendor's data.
- Colebrook as an implicit law in f (second implicit unknown).
- A choice of damper setting (a slot with a continuous value) — only "all settings" via a range.
- "At least 60 % open" as a constraint linking the damper position to K — K range given by hand.
- A fan-curve table (x,y points) as data: I converted to line coefficients by hand in the generator.
- Asking "what minimum diameter satisfies 8 m/s" (answer: 441 mm; I computed it outside).

## TOOL OUTPUT
Run command exactly as required, on `examples/blind2/c11.json`: **REFUTED**, 135 s (22 MB; earlier identical run
about 6 min while the machine was loaded). `designs_tried 15, failed_at_nominal 15, judged 0`. Every design fails the
velocity requirement at the nominal point. `closest_design` D400/F1 (the stand's tie-break), witness `Qd = 4000`,
`vmax = 8.84194 m/s`, overshoot 0.841941. `spread_over_tolerances` for vmax [7.958, 9.726]. `minimal_conflicts`:
one set, the velocity requirement alone. Verdict is correct but only the velocity limit was ever tested: the other
requirements, the cost and the operating point were not exercised by this run.

Variants run on scratch files (same command, scratchpad paths; generator `gen11.py` in the scratchpad):
- **First try, `min` of 4 lines inside an implicit law for Q:** `STAND ERROR ... NoDerivative: min has no derivative
  where its arguments cross (at zcertify.py:99)` after 12 s. A genuine stand error, not a verdict.
- **B10 (final structure, velocity limit relaxed to 10 m/s):** EARNED in 2.2 s. Duct 400 mm, fan F2 (c1=900, s1=3/100,
  c2=980, s2=7/100 ...). Worst-case lifetime cost proved <= 5517.76, reached >= 5502.07, `optimal: true`. All 12 designs
  with D < 400 refuted by velocity, the three 400 mm designs EARNED (F1 5553.9, F2 5502.1, F3 5761.4 reached values).
  `m10`, `m36`, `Pw` judged "interval reading of the definition (sound, may be wide)"; `Pw` "outside ZTL: interval bound".
- **AB10 (B10 plus an implicit Q with the fan as a smooth quartic through its 5 points, exact rational coefficients):**
  468 s. EARNED, same winner; Q for 400/F2 in [5180.9, 5703.8] m3/h "root-exclusion certificate checked by the kernel".
  But `optimal: false`, "1 design OPEN": 400 mm + F3, "the checker could not evaluate it: definitions depend on each
  other in a cycle or on an unknown" — a misleading message; in reality the F3 curve has no crossing below 6000
  m3/h at some corners (my check: g(6000) > 0 at some corners), i.e. an honest "no root in search" case.
- **AB8 (the real 8 m/s limit with the implicit Q in the brick):** **OPEN** after 94 s, not REFUTED. 14 designs refuted
  at nominal; 400 mm + F3 was not dropped (nominal operating point not computable) and ended OPEN with the same
  misleading message. So one design with an unevaluable implicit law keeps the whole question OPEN even though its
  velocity violation depends on givens only and is decidable. This is why the final brick has no implicit Q.
- Reported ranges for Q: the `how` line shows the checker's enclosure `['1.48069','1.54749']` in m3/s while
  `guaranteed` shows Q in m3/h (units of the two lines differ).

## DOES THE ANSWER LOOK RIGHT
Checked in python (`chk11.py`, scratchpad; own Colebrook to convergence, own piecewise-linear interpolation, 27 corners of
T x damper x flexible-roughness):
- Velocity: D = 250/280/315/355/400 mm gives 24.9/19.85/15.68/12.35/9.73 m/s at 4400 and 22.6/18.0/14.3/11.2/8.84 at
  4000. Stand witness 8.84194 for D400 at 4000 and upper spread 9.726 match. Minimum diameter for 8 m/s: 400*sqrt(9.73/8) = 441 mm,
  so no catalog duct qualifies. **REFUTED is right.**
- Relaxed variant: my offline worst corner lifetime cost 400/F1 5554, 400/F2 5502, 400/F3 5761 vs the stand's
  reached values 5553.9 / 5502.07 / 5761.4: match to 3-4 digits. With 10 m/s only D400 passes velocity.
- Margin at 4000: my minimum m10 for 400/F2 = 347 Pa; stand's guaranteed range [314, 464]: consistent (outer interval
  wider, as announced). Pw max 572 W (mine) vs stand upper 574.3: match. m36 min 453.9 (mine) vs stand lower 429.6: consistent.
- Operating point (400/F2): mine with the vendor piecewise-linear curve 5309-5543, with the quartic 5330-5569; stand
  enclosure [5181, 5704]: contains both (outer, sound).
- Not checked: the stand's certificates themselves; ranges for the 12 designs that were dropped at nominal;
  whether Colebrook truncation matters at corners other than the 12 samples; damper/length interpretations (they are
  my readings, not facts).
- Side result of my own calculation (not from the stand): with no velocity limit, the cheapest feasible pairs are
  355 mm + F2/F3 ($7576 / $7760 worst case) and 400 mm + F2 ($5502); 315 mm + F3 passes the margin by only 2.9 Pa and 1575 W.

## DOC GAPS
- BRICKS.md says an implicit law "may use functions", but does not say that `min`/`max` inside an implicit law crash
  (`NoDerivative`); a concave piecewise-linear curve is the commonest way to write a fan/pump curve.
- No guidance on how to carry a tabulated curve (x,y points) — the natural idiom (min of lines) fails in implicit laws.
- The OPEN message "definitions depend on each other in a cycle or on an unknown" is shown for a case that is really
  "no root in `search` for some corners" (inferred, not confirmed); the doc lists the "implicit law without a root" reason
  but the output did not use it.
- Nothing says that a single OPEN design (undecidable implicit law at nominal) blocks a REFUTED verdict even when other
  requirements refute it from givens alone.
- Not stated whether the nominal pre-filter evaluates the implicit law (it seems to: `failed_at_nominal` differs between
  B8 and AB8), nor that requirements are tried in listed order at nominal (the velocity line was always the reported failure).
- `Hrs*Ce*Pw` style unit combos (`USD/(kWh)`, `Pa*h/m^3`) work but "h" as a unit versus the prefix `h` is not stated to
  be safe; it was fine here.
- `"1.46/100000"` is rejected as a number although `"1/3"` is documented; only plain decimals or `a/b` with integers.
- The guide says `C` has no offset and "25" in `(T-25)` is a dimensionless number; but `/25` in a product with T in C made
  a unit error (`m^2*s^-1 vs m^2*s^-1*K`) — a divisor that is a temperature span must be a given with unit C.
- Run time is hard to predict: the same B8 brick took 135 s and about 6 min; a single implicit-law design costs minutes.
