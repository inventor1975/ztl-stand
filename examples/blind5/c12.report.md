# c12 report: Call-centre staffing and self-service option (problem 12)

Verdict: EARNED, optimal. Cheapest design: n = (25, 29, 22, 13) agents in P1..P4, IVR "advanced". Annual cost 2992 kEUR.
Brick: examples/blind5/c12.json (generated by examples/blind5/work_c12/gen.py, `python3 gen.py full ../c12.json`).

## EXPRESSED FAITHFULLY
- Arrivals lambda_s: four independent toleranced ranges, unit 1/h. AHT: one range 4.8..5.6 min, one quantity shared by all parts. Deflection d: one catalog field of the IVR item (range 0.06..0.10 or 0.12..0.18, exactly 0 for "none"), so d is ONE value common to all parts and tied to the chosen option. The "shared" semantics came out right without any trick.
- Offered load A_s = lambda_s (1-d) AHT (units convert the /60). Occupancy = A/n. Service level SL = 1 - P_wait * exp(-(n-A)*Tw/AHT), with Tw = 20 s as a given quantity with unit s, AHT in min (so "60 AHT is seconds" is done by the unit system, no conversion factor in a law).
- Objective: (n1 c1 + .. + n4 c4)*Hyr + IVR.price, with c_s EUR/h, Hyr = 900 h (3 h x 300 days), price in kEUR, reported in kEUR.
- R1 (SL_s >= 0.70), R3 (occupancy <= 0.92) as stated, per part, over the whole box.
- Case n <= A (SL = 0): the Erlang formula is guarded by a `valid` condition n > A on the P_wait law. Outside it the design is REFUTED, which agrees with the statement (SL = 0 fails R1).
- n is a real integer slot per part (integers catalog), the IVR option is a real catalog choice.

## APPROXIMATED
1. **Erlang C rewritten by hand.** The sum over k<n has variable length, which no law can say. I used the inverse Erlang-B recursion X_k = 1 + (k/A) X_{k-1}, X_0 = 1 (one derived quantity per k, with `if(k > n, X_{k-1}, step)` so steps beyond n do nothing), then P_wait = 1/(1 + (1 - A/n)(X_n - 1)). This is the same number (I checked it against the sum form from the statement at four points: they agree to 1e-15). Safe, but the identity is checked only by me; the stand cannot see that it is Erlang C. The chain is 28/32/26/17 derived quantities per part, the brick file is about 34 KB of JSON.
2. **R2 rewritten by hand.** SL_day >= 0.87 became M = sum_s lambda_s (SL_s - 0.87) >= 0: divided out the common positive factor (1-d), which also removes the weights' dependence on d. Equivalent. Checked only by me. Side effect: the stand's interval reading of M loses the correlation between lambda_s in the weight and in SL_s, so its guaranteed lower bound for M at the chosen design is 1.0175 1/h while my own scan gives about 4.6. The loss is on the sound side (it can only make the stand say "not proved"), but it could in principle leave a truly feasible neighbouring design OPEN; here none was OPEN.
3. **Catalog windows for n_s computed outside the stand** (the 1..60 range would be 60^4 x 3 designs): n1 22..28, n2 27..32, n3 21..26, n4 13..17. Lower ends = the minimal n that meets R1 and R3 at the worst corner under the best IVR (advanced, d = 0.12), found by my own python Erlang calculation; they are valid for every IVR option because a smaller d needs more agents. Upper ends = cost argument: any design with some n_s above the window costs more than 2992 kEUR even if all other parts sit at their lower ends and the IVR is the cheapest per-agent combination. Both are checked only by me (the lower ends by my python; the stand later refuted every cheaper design inside the window, but it did not look outside it). NOT conservative in principle if my python lower ends are wrong; I believe they are right (the stand's own numbers for SL1..SL4 at the optimum match mine to 12 digits). R4 (n <= 60) holds because the window is inside 1..60; it is not a requirement in the brick.
4. Hours per year Hyr = 900 h typed as a given (3 x 300), cost per part c_s given as exact values with ground "problem statement".
5. valid_i conditions use the givens directly (n > lambda (1-d) AHT) instead of A_s, to avoid a derived quantity inside the condition. Same meaning.

## COULD NOT EXPRESS
- A sum or recursion whose length depends on a chosen integer (Erlang C directly). Only the unrolled-with-`if` chain worked, with the maximum length fixed by hand.
- The weighted average SL_day as a ratio with the correlation between its own weights and its terms kept. I had to turn it into a difference form, and the stand still reads it loosely (see 2).
- "Search all n in 1..60" with the stand's design enumeration: the product of four 60-item catalogs is out of reach, so the windows (3) had to be computed outside.
- Nothing in the format states "the optimum lies inside this window": that claim is mine.

## DOC GAPS
- BRICKS.md does not say that `integers` catalogs of different slots need different catalog names (I used one per slot); it also does not say how a variable-length sum/recursion could be expressed. The `if(k > n, ...)` with a number against a slot field works but is nowhere shown.
- Whether a bare `0.87` or `20` inside a law is dimensionless or SI seconds is only derivable from the pitfall paragraph; I avoided it with given quantities.
- `valid` is documented, but not whether its expression may use slot fields (it can) or that it appears in `requirements` and `guaranteed` as `valid_<index>` where the index counts laws, not valid conditions.
- No indication of run time per design or an estimate for large catalogs; the full run took 792 s of a 900 s cap. A progress line only reaches stderr and the JSON appears at the end.
- The doc says "a derived quantity equal to a product of toleranced quantities is bounded outside ZTL" but does not warn that the weighted-average form M loses correlation and gives a bound four times looser than the truth.
