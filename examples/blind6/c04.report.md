## APPROXIMATED
1. R1 "at the LOWEST supply voltage" and R2 "at the HIGHEST": I did not pin V to 207 / 253. V stays a range for both
   requirements. Equivalent because P(V) is increasing in V (R1 worst at 207, R2 worst at 253). Safe; the stand does
   the check (it found the V=207 / V=253 corners itself), the monotonicity argument is mine.
2. R1 is written as `margin == Pc - q`, `margin >= 0` (a bound may not be a derived quantity). Exact, no loss.
3. R3 as `D2 == 2*r2`, `D2 <= 180 mm`. Exact.
4. The limit as a window: `find smallest` judges the range [a, -35] C and needs "works for EVERY Ta in it". The
   problem asks "works AT Ta". These coincide only because colder is harder (q rises as Ta falls; nothing else depends on
   Ta). Not checked by the stand; I argued it and checked with my python. The upper end -35 and lower end -45 are my
   choices. The reported limit is on a 0.01 grid (proved -37.86, beyond -37.87), not the exact -37.8623. Safe
   (proved side is on the conservative side).
5. The decision at Ta = -40 and the limit are two bricks, not one (see COULD NOT EXPRESS). The design for the limit
   question is the cheapest design that works on the whole window [-37.86, -35]; it is the only design at the limit
   itself (my python), but the stand does not say "the design at the limit".
6. `pi` is the stand's 1e-15 bracket (immaterial).
