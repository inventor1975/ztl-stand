# c11 report: Fleet insurance, deductible + stop-loss + reserve (problem 11)

Brick: `examples/blind6/c11.json` (flat, 20 discrete combinations x one continuous amount R). Scratch, variants, outputs and
my own checker: `examples/blind6/work_c11/` (`gen11.py` builds the brick, `check11.py` is my corner calculation).
Chain of devices: no, so no components; one flat brick only.

Verdict of the faithful brick: **OPEN after 9 min 55 s** (19 of 20 combinations proved to have no mix, one not decided).
My own calculation says the true answer is **REFUTED**: no admissible design; R1 and R4 conflict; dropping either one is enough.

## EXPRESSED FAITHFULLY
- D and premium from the table (5 catalog items, one slot `DED`); stop-loss none/SL60/SL100/SL160 with A, limit, price (slot `SL`);
  R continuous >= 0 (slot `RES`, catalog `continuous`).
- f = 1.00..1.08 is ONE given `f`, used in all three year-costs. Premium is `f*DED.prem` as stated.
- Severities and claim counts as ranges (givens with `range`), one set per scenario, so the three years are independent
  (R4 compares the bad year against the normal year with independent data, as "independently" asks).
- Laws exactly as stated: `r_s == nS*min(sS, D) + nL*min(sL, D) [+ nC*min(sC, D)]`, `rp_s == r_s - min(max(r_s - A, 0), lim)`,
  `T_s == f*prem + SLprice + rp_s + RES.x/20`. The `min`/`max` kinks are written as functions in laws; no hand algebra.
- Requirements R1, R2, R5 directly on `T_n`, `T_b`, `S == T_n+T_b+T_c`; R3 as `M3 == rp_c - RES.x <= 40`;
  R4 as `D4 == rp_b - rp_n within [-62, 62]`. All units kEUR; the unit check passed ("every law's dimensions agree").
- The stop-loss "none" is an item with limit 0, price 0 (A = 0): then `rp == r`.

## APPROXIMATED
1. **Objective is NOMINAL, not worst-case.** `"case": "worst"` with a continuous slot is a BRICK ERROR ("continuous choices need a
   nominal objective"), so the brick minimizes S at the nominal (midpoint) data. This is NOT "minimum worst-case sum". The worst-case
   sum is therefore not an output of the stand. Not conservative for the stated aim; I checked the worst-case optimum myself
   (`check11.py`) and only used the stand for yes/no questions "S <= B holds" with hand-set B (outer bisection by me, 2 steps per variant).
2. **R upper bound 1000 kEUR** (the stand needs a box). Safe: R3 is the only lower bound on R and needs at most 520; costs rise with R.
   Checked by me only.
3. **Claim counts are continuous** (the problem says "any value in the range"), not integers. Superset of integers, conservative for the
   worst case (all monotone). One severity per claim class instead of one per claim: equal for the worst case because every term is monotone in
   each severity; this equivalence is my argument, not checked by the stand.
4. **The catastrophic count is absent in normal/bad years** (law term dropped since the count is exactly 0) and fixed at exactly 1 in the
   catastrophe year. Same thing as stated.
5. **Bound-by-bisection**: to find the minimum worst-case sum I could not use `find` (see COULD NOT EXPRESS); I set the R5 bound by hand
   (631/630 and 646/645) on the two relaxed variants `c11_noR4_S*.json`, `c11_noR1_S*.json`. The relaxations (R4 or R1 deleted) are mine; the
   stand judges each, but the choice of which requirement to drop and the bisection are mine.

## COULD NOT EXPRESS
- **"Minimize the worst-case sum"** with a continuous reserve: `case: worst` is refused (see above). Workaround 5 above.
- **`find` for the smallest guaranteed sum**: tried `find: {"smallest": "Bsum", ...}` with `S <= Bsum` (a given as bound). The stand's first
  try on the continuous brick returned BRICK ERROR: "requirement on S: in continuous choices a bound is a number or an expression of exact
  values of the combination (version 1): 'Bsum'". So the searched quantity cannot be a bound in a brick with a continuous slot (outputs
  `work_c11/out_c11_noR4_find.txt`, `out_c11_noR1_find.txt`).
- Integer claim counts; separate severity per claim (both only matter if worst case is not monotone, here it is monotone).
- "Whether dropping one requirement is enough" needs `minimal_conflicts`; the stand did not produce it (verdict OPEN, not REFUTED). I got the
  conflict sets by my own calculation plus relaxed bricks (see below).

## DOES THE ANSWER LOOK RIGHT
`work_c11/check11.py`: exact fractions; every quantity is non-decreasing in every datum, so worst case = upper corner for costs and R3,
R4 = (bad upper) - (normal lower) and the reverse; premium at f = 1.08; R = max(0, r'_c,max - 40) (the smallest R that satisfies R3, costs only rise
with R). For the 20 designs:
- Designs passing R1..R5 together: none. Per-design: D20+SL60 fails only R4 (r'_b max 148, r'_n min 40, difference 108 > 62); D10+SL60 fails
  only R4 (118 - 40 = 78); D5+SL60 fails only R1 (215.24 > 210); D20+SL100 fails only R4. Others fail more.
- Minimal conflicting set: {R1, R4}. Dropping R1 alone: best is D5+SL60, R = 20, worst sum 645.72. Dropping R4 alone: D20+SL60, R = 60,
  worst sum 630.68. Dropping R2, R3 or R5 alone: still no design (my calculation, not run in the stand).
- Stand vs mine: 19/20 combinations proved without mix in the faithful brick (agrees: all 20 fail, the stand has only not decided D10+SL60,
  and alone it decides it in 82 s); R4-dropped with bound 631 EARNED D20+SL60 R = 60.0001 (agrees: 630.68 <= 631 and the R sits on its
  kink 60); bound 630 not refuted but OPEN (agrees with truth only in being not-EARNED; truth is infeasible, not decided by the stand);
  R1-dropped with bound 646: EARNED with R = 20.0001, matching D5+SL60 (645.72) in R but printed under the wrong deductible (see above).
- D40+SL60 witness (M3 = 130) checked by hand above.
- Not checked: the stand's `guaranteed` ranges (their lower ends are not meaningful, e.g. T_n lower -67.5; only the upper side is a proof);
  the premium-sharing "ONE f" correlation in the stand's proof; the Farkas weights.
